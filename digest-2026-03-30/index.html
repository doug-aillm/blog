Daily Digest - March 30, 2026

Monday: BTC $65K open, Houthis widen the war, oil $115, Hyperliquid's Tokyo latency edge, BlackRock goes all-in on crypto talent

Monday market open. Liberation Day countdown begins in earnest.

Three days until April 2 and the tariff hammer drops. Add an active Iran war to the mix and you get last Friday’s bloodbath - BTC down to $65,720 intraday, the lowest since March 2. Not a great setup heading into the week. Let’s dig in.

🔑 Top Stories

Strategy, BitMine and Robinhood Shares Hit Monthly Lows as Bitcoin Sinks Further - BTC closed Friday around $65,800, down 4%+, with $500M in liquidations in 24 hours - nearly 90% from longs. Israel’s “escalate” response after Trump paused the Iran assault killed the relief rally before it started. Prediction markets are now 64% in the $55K camp over $84K. That’s a sharp sentiment flip from just Thursday morning.

Strategy May Have Paused Bitcoin Accumulation Last Week, Ending a 13-Week Buying Streak - First time since late December that Saylor’s crew didn’t file a weekly BTC buy. Could be timing, could be intentional at these price levels, could be a sign the infinite money machine has hiccups. Worth watching this week’s SEC filings closely.

Bitcoin Bullish Bets Hit a 28-Month High on Bitfinex - and That’s Music to Bears’ Ears - Bitfinex long/short ratio hitting extremes has historically been a reliable fade signal. When retail and semi-institutional longs pile in at this level, the market tends to punish them. I’m not calling $55K today, but this is not the positioning you want to see if you’re structurally long.

Markets Move to Price In Rate Hikes as Inflation Fears and Geopolitics Reshape Fed Expectations - Oil elevated, Middle East hot, traders now pricing ~40% chance of zero rate cuts this year. The macro thesis for BTC as a risk-on asset is getting stress-tested in real time. Liberation Day tariffs on April 2 add another inflationary input. Not a fun backdrop.

Crypto’s CLARITY Act Could Be a Headwind for DeFi Tokens Ring-Fencing Yield, Analyst Says - 10x Research’s Markus Thielen flags that if the CLARITY Act restricts yield to regulated entities, value shifts away from DeFi protocol tokens toward compliant platforms. I’ve been bullish on the regulatory clarity narrative but this is the flip side nobody wants to talk about. DeFi governance tokens would take a structural hit.

No One Is 100% Happy with the Stablecoin Yield Agreement: State of Crypto - Senators Alsobrooks and Tillis reached an “agreement-in-principle” on stablecoin yield under the GENIUS Act, and predictably both sides are grumbling. Banking lobby hates it, pure DeFi crowd hates it differently. Welcome to sausage-making. A half-measure that passes is still miles better than nothing.

The 7 Largest Publicly Traded Ethereum Treasury Firms - Public firms now hold 7.3M ETH - over 6% of circulating supply. BitMine at 4.6M ETH, Sharplink at 869K. These numbers are staggering. The ETH treasury playbook is working at a scale nobody predicted 18 months ago. Tom Lee’s $60K ETH call looks ridiculous right now with ETH at $1,980, but the structural accumulation is very real.

Inside Aave’s Governance Battle as DeFi Giant Prepares for Upgrade - Stani Kulechov talking openly about governance tensions ahead of a major Aave upgrade is notable. Aave has been one of the cleanest DeFi protocols operationally, so when the CEO starts airing governance complexity, it means the upgrade matters. Watch how the community votes - it’s a signal for the broader DeFi governance meta.

Bittensor Ecosystem Tokens’ Value Hit $1.5 Billion as Jensen Huang Endorsement Supports TAO Rally - Subnet tokens posting 200-400% monthly gains off Jensen’s nod. The AI/crypto convergence trade is real - Bittensor sits at the intersection where NVIDIA’s narrative meets decentralized compute. Highly speculative subnet tokens are playing out as leveraged TAO bets. Position accordingly.

Stablecoin Payments Go ‘Invisible’ in Southeast Asia as Crypto Card Business Surges - StraitsX reporting 40x transaction volume surge and 83x increase in card issuance from 2024 to 2025. Southeast Asia is doing it right - not hyping crypto, just making stablecoins the boring payment rail underneath existing UX. This is how adoption actually happens. The infrastructure is maturing faster than the Western narrative gives it credit for.

Monday close. The war widened, BTC found its footing, and BlackRock made the institutional signal of the quarter.

Asia opened ugly. Houthis entered the Iran war theater overnight, Nikkei -3.4%, Korean markets -3.2%, oil at $115. BTC briefly hit $65,112 - its lowest since the war started - then recovered to $67,400 by mid-morning. The higher low pattern from the previous four escalation cycles broke for the first time. Whether that’s noise or the beginning of a trend change is the story for the rest of the week. Liberation Day is still 3 days out.

🔑 Top Stories

Bitcoin Recovers to $67,400 After Dipping Below $65,200 as Houthis Enter Iran War - The war entered its fifth week and its widest expansion yet. Houthi forces opened a new front, additional U.S. troops landed in the region, and Iran attacked two aluminum production sites - sending aluminum up 6%. Oil is now at $115, up roughly 90% year-to-date. The inflationary shock is broadening from energy into industrial supply chains, which is exactly the environment where rate cut expectations get crushed. Tron was the only major sitting green (+4.6% on the week), which tells you something about where the flight-to-alternative flows are going.

Hyperliquid Traders in Tokyo Get 200-Millisecond Edge, Glassnode Research Shows - Glassnode’s Hyperlatency tool put hard numbers on something the market has suspected: Hyperliquid’s 24 validators sit in AWS ap-northeast-1 (Tokyo), giving Tokyo traders a 2-3ms round trip vs 200ms+ for European traders. BitMEX ran the same playbook before Hyperliquid - moved from Ireland to Tokyo, got +180% liquidity and up to +400% in altcoin markets. The decentralization narrative is real, but equal access physically isn’t. Worth knowing if you’re building or trading.

BlackRock Adds $350K Head of Digital Assets Role - Managing Director of Digital Assets, NYC, $350K base. BlackRock is the world’s largest asset manager. This isn’t a PR hire - it’s infrastructure build-out. The person sitting in that seat will run crypto, stablecoin, and tokenization strategy for a $10T+ firm. When the biggest institutional allocator is paying Tier 1 comp for crypto leadership, the on-ramp isn’t a maybe. It’s being built right now.

Washington Sues Kalshi as States Ramp Up Legal Pressure Against Prediction Markets - WA state AG calls Kalshi’s products “gambling dressed up as prediction markets.” This is the opposite direction from the federal CFTC trend where Kalshi just got a margin trading license for institutional clients. Classic federal-state regulatory arbitrage. Kalshi is caught between a federal regulator giving it more tools and state AGs treating it as a lottery operator. The legal geography question is unresolved.

New Ethereum Project Aims to Fix Network Fragmentation and Improve User Experience - The L2 fragmentation problem is Ethereum’s biggest UX liability right now. Bridging across Arbitrum, Optimism, Base, zkSync, Scroll, Linea is a nightmare for normal users. A protocol-level fix that makes the many L2s feel like one network is the missing layer. No details on implementation yet, but the problem is real - watch this.

DEX Volume Hits $12.5 Trillion in Q1 as DeFi Defies Bear Sentiment - $12.5T in DEX volume during a quarter that included Extreme Fear readings, a shooting war, and BTC at multi-month lows. The liquidity didn’t leave DeFi - it rotated within it. On-chain activity is increasingly decoupled from token price sentiment. That’s the infrastructure maturity signal the market keeps missing because it’s not measured by green candles.

CLARITY Act Markup Vote Reportedly Set for April 10-16 in Senate Banking Committee - Unconfirmed source, parody account, treat with caution - but the timeline is consistent with Lummis’s statement that text drops “this week.” If markup is April 10-16, that’s a faster track than most expected. The “code vs control” legal test in that bill is what the DeFi industry actually needs. Watch for the official Senate announcement.

Quantum Threat Preparedness Across Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana - The major L1s are diverging on quantum prep. Bitcoin is moving slowly (social consensus-heavy), Ethereum is doing more active cryptography research, Solana is somewhere in between. The quantum timeline from NIST puts “harvest now, decrypt later” attacks as a realistic threat within 10 years. Not urgent today, but worth understanding which chains are taking it seriously.

Lido DAO Proposes One-Time $20M LDO Token Buyback - Lido burning $20M worth of LDO from DAO treasury. With ETH at $2,044 and Lido holding 28% of staked ETH, the protocol generates substantial fee revenue. This is protocol maturity signaling - “we have enough cash flow to buy back tokens” is not a message DAOs sent in 2021. The governance meta for staking protocols is quietly shifting from growth-at-all-costs to sustainable economics.

Bitcoin’s “Compressed” Valuation Offers Reduced Downside vs Stocks - With BTC down 47% from its $126K peak and S&P still near highs, the relative valuation argument actually holds. BTC has already priced in the macro pain. Equities haven’t. The ETF flows, sovereign reserve accumulation, and regulatory clarity pipeline all continue regardless of short-term price. The “compressed valuation” framing is a better way to think about where we are than “crypto bear market.”

RLUSD Surpasses $1B Market Cap in First Year as Stablecoin Institutionalization Era Begins - Ripple’s regulated stablecoin crossing $1B within its first year is underrated. RLUSD is doing what PYUSD couldn’t - building institutional distribution through Ripple’s existing payments network. The stablecoin landscape is bifurcating: USDT for retail/offshore, USDC/RLUSD for regulated institutional flows. North America is driving the institutional wave.
