Morning Edition

BTC touched $75K yesterday and is now pulling back to $74,200 in the Asia session. That’s not a crash - that’s consolidation. The question is whether institutional follow-through shows up in today’s US session or we get another week of range-bound noise before the BoJ meeting on April 28.

Meanwhile the quantum debate is getting louder. And Allbirds just pivoted from shoes to GPUs, which is either genius or peak clown. Let’s dig in.

1. Bitcoin Quantum Freeze Proposal Gets Formal (BIP 361)

What: A new Bitcoin Improvement Proposal called “Post Quantum Migration and Legacy Signature Sunset” would phase out legacy signature schemes and freeze coins that don’t migrate to quantum-resistant addresses. That includes Satoshi’s estimated 1.1M BTC in early P2PK outputs.

Why it matters: This isn’t fringe anymore - it’s on Bitcoin’s official repository. The proposal would stop users from sending to older address types, then disable the cryptographic methods Bitcoin has used since day one. Migration window would give holders time to move, but unclaimed wallets (including dormant ones) would be frozen.

The tension: Immutability is a core Bitcoin principle. Freezing coins - even quantum-vulnerable ones - is a precedent with no clean off-ramp. Expect this debate to run for years.

Source: CoinDesk Tech | Decrypt

2. DoubleZero: Private Fiber for DeFi’s Latency Problem

What: Ex-Solana exec building DoubleZero, a private fiber network designed to eliminate latency advantages in DeFi. The target: setups like Hyperliquid’s Tokyo co-location edge that lets certain players see and react to trades milliseconds faster than others.

Why it matters: This is Wall Street market microstructure applied to onchain. In TradFi, co-lo access at NYSE is tightly regulated. In DeFi it’s the wild west - whoever pays for proximity wins. DoubleZero wants to level that. Exchanges haven’t shown interest yet, but that’s the sell.

Source: CoinDesk Tech

3. AI Models Can’t Beat Sports Betting - All 8 Lost Money

What: General Reasoning ran 8 frontier models - Claude, Grok, Gemini, GPT-5.4 - through a full Premier League season betting challenge using the Kelly criterion. Every single model lost money. Several went bankrupt.

Why it matters: KellyBench is a clean benchmark: it tests whether models can identify genuine edges in a market where the margin is <2%. None could. This matters for the AI trading thesis - financial markets aren’t solved by raw reasoning, and the gap between “impressive on benchmarks” and “profitable under adversarial conditions” is real.

Source: Decrypt

Why it matters: This is either brilliant capital allocation in a compute-scarce world, or the most 2026 headline possible. The pivot follows a pattern: distressed public companies with cheap capital structures are increasingly buying their way into AI infrastructure. Watch for more.

Source: Decrypt

5. Elizabeth Warren Targets X Money Before April Launch

Why it matters: Every major TradFi institution has the same objection to public blockchains: “we can’t show our book to competitors.” ZK solves that. XRPL adding ZK puts it on the institutional shortlist alongside private chains. Timing aligns with the wave of bank custody filings.

1. Adam Back vs. Jameson Lopp: Bitcoin’s Quantum Civil War

What: One day after Jameson Lopp proposed BIP-361 (freeze old coins, force migration), Adam Back pushed back at Paris Blockchain Week. Back’s position: optional quantum-resistant upgrades, not forced sunset of legacy signatures. Lopp says optional isn’t enough.

Why it matters: This is the real debate. Back’s view respects immutability - no one loses coins involuntarily. Lopp’s view says optional adoption won’t reach 100%, leaving the network vulnerable. Both are right about different things. The resolution will define Bitcoin’s relationship with protocol change for a generation.
