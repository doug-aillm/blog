## 1. Ledger Investigated Suspected CryptoBilis Wallet Drains

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-10-09-ledger-cryptobilis-fund-losses-418163) that Ledger is investigating reports of fund losses from Southeast Asian users who bought devices from reseller CryptoBilis.

Ledger asked CryptoBilis to pause sales and shipments while it investigates. The company also told users who bought from that reseller in the past 90 days not to set up unused devices, and told users who already set them up to consider moving assets to a new signer with a new seed.

Onchain estimates cited by The Block ranged from more than $72 million to more than $86 million across Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Tron, though the totals were unconfirmed.

This is the most important security story of the morning.

Cold storage is only as clean as the path from factory to user. If a reseller, package, firmware path, seed ceremony, or support process gets compromised, "self-custody" stops being a slogan and becomes an incident-response problem with almost no customer-service fallback.

## 2. Blockchain.com Asked For A U.S. Prediction-Market And Derivatives Lane

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-10-09-blockchain-com-cftc-license-prediction-markets-derivatives-418192) that Blockchain.com applied for a designated contract market license and futures commission merchant registration.

The company already offers prediction markets through Polymarket and perpetual futures through Hyperliquid to some non-U.S. users. If the CFTC approves the applications, Blockchain.com could bring those products to U.S. customers inside one app. The filing also lands while Blockchain.com is reportedly targeting a $4 billion to $6 billion IPO valuation.

This turns the prediction-market fight into a distribution fight.

Kalshi and Polymarket made the category visible. Blockchain.com is asking whether a wallet and trading app can bundle event contracts, perps, spot crypto, and tokenized stocks under regulated U.S. rails. The answer matters because users don't want separate apps for every market primitive.

## 3. Zcash Developers Targeted January For Quantum-Resistant Payments

[CoinDesk reported](https://www.coindesk.com/fil/tech/2026/10/09/zcash-developers-set-january-target-for-quantum-resistant-payments-after-bunker-mode-scare) that Zcash developers are aiming to add support for quantum-resistant, hash-based signatures in January, though no network activation date is confirmed.

The first target is transparent Zcash payments. CoinDesk calculated that about 11.96 million of 16.98 million issued ZEC were in the transparent pool on Thursday, roughly seven out of 10 coins. Shielded payments need separate protection.

This is better than panic.

The "bunker mode" debate can sound like science fiction until teams start naming surfaces, dates, and migration paths. Public-key exposure is not the same as a live exploit, but it is a real inventory problem. Chains that can quantify exposed funds and ship incremental mitigations will look more credible than chains that answer every key-risk question with vibes.

## 4. The U.S. Said It May Seize $1 Billion In Iran-Linked Crypto

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-10-09-us-targets-1-billion-iran-linked-crypto-seizure-bessent-says-we-know-where-it-is-418151) that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the U.S. would "probably" seize $1 billion in Iran-linked crypto this week.

Bessent did not identify the wallets or clarify whether the amount is new money or part of earlier seizure and freeze actions. The Block noted that Treasury has sanctioned several Iranian crypto exchanges since June, including BitBank in September and Nobitex, Wallex, Bitpin, Ramzinex, Shelbit, and Aban Tether earlier in the year.

This is sanctions policy moving through wallets, exchanges, and issuers.

The operational lesson is simple: blacklists, stablecoin freezes, forensic clustering, and exchange sanctions now sit inside the same enforcement machine. Crypto rails don't remove jurisdiction. They give investigators a different map.

## 5. New York Secured Up To $35 Million From Celsius' Alex Mashinsky

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-10-09-ny-attorney-general-alex-mashinsky-celsius-35-million-ban-418154) that New York Attorney General Letitia James secured up to $35 million from former Celsius CEO Alex Mashinsky and a permanent ban from the securities, commodities, and crypto industries.

Mashinsky is already serving a 12-year federal prison sentence. Celsius creditors have received more than $3.4 billion through the bankruptcy process, according to The Block.

This is the lending-cycle hangover still resolving.

The market has moved on to tokenized stocks, Bitcoin insurance, and onchain credit vaults. Courts and regulators are still closing the last credit boom. That matters because today's yield products will eventually be judged by the same basic questions: what did customers think they were promised, where did the assets go, and who had the power to hide the risk?

## 6. Base's Tokenization Pitch Shifted Toward Non-Dollar Stablecoins

[The Block interviewed](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-10-09-tokenization-supercycle-base-jesse-pollak-418144) Base creator Jesse Pollak, who said tokenized equities and non-dollar stablecoins will lead the next tokenization cycle.

Pollak said Base now has 32 stablecoins across 21 currencies. He also said Coinbase's tokenized-stock rollout on Base is seeing about $70 million to $100 million of daily trading volume, with 50 stocks listed and 250 expected by the end of October.

This is a useful sequel to yesterday's Solana and Securitize stock-token story.

The chain competition is no longer just "who can list equities?" It is who can pair equities, local-currency stablecoins, payments, financing, and trading APIs into something fintechs can use without forcing end users to think about chains.

## 7. Bitcoin Life Insurer Meanwhile Raised $37.5 Million

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/deals/2026-10-09-sam-altman-backed-bitcoin-life-insurer-meanwhile-raises-37-5-million-round-led-by-bain-capital-crypto-418131) that Meanwhile raised $37.5 million from existing investors led by Bain Capital Crypto, bringing total funding above $180 million.

The Bermuda-based company sells bitcoin-denominated life insurance and has signed 15 broker partners serving wealthy families in markets including Singapore, Hong Kong, the UAE, and Switzerland. Its BTC Life 1-Pay product requires a single bitcoin premium, pays a bitcoin death benefit, and lets policyholders borrow up to 90% of policy value after the first year.

This is Bitcoin as estate-planning collateral.

Yesterday's lending story was tuition and working capital. Meanwhile pushes the same idea into wealth transfer. The open question is whether wealthy holders want Bitcoin exposure wrapped in insurance law badly enough to accept custody, actuarial, regulatory, and liquidity constraints.

## 8. A $14.35 Million Pool Drain Bypassed Burned LP Receipts

[CryptoSlate reported](https://cryptoslate.com/79thvaults-14-million-pool-drain-shows-why-locked-liquidity-can-fail/) that Bitquery traced a $14.35 million USDT drain from 79thVault's 79AU pool on PancakeSwap.

Bitquery found that 79% of the pool's LP receipts had been burned, but a token permission let 79AU leave the pool without payment and then be sold back for USDT. CryptoSlate also reported that Bitquery's October 8 snapshot found two addresses with pull rights over remaining 79AU.

This is the ugly difference between liquidity optics and token controls.

Burned LP receipts can stop ordinary redemption. They don't revoke token-level privileges. That distinction is exactly where retail dashboards, lock badges, and "liquidity burned" marketing can create false comfort.

## 9. XRPL's Stablecoin Base Did Not Prove XRP Demand

[CryptoSlate analyzed](https://cryptoslate.com/xrpls-1-34-billion-stablecoin-base-doesnt-tell-us-how-much-xrp-users-need/) XRPL's $1.338 billion tracked stablecoin base and argued that the supply figure doesn't directly measure incremental XRP demand.

Fees and account reserves require XRP, but stablecoins, tokenized funds, and securities records can keep principal in other assets. The harder measurements are how much activity routes through XRP, how much XRP sits in liquidity pools or lending vaults, and how long that inventory stays committed.

This is a good antidote to lazy chain metrics.

Stablecoin supply is a balance. DEX volume is turnover. Fees are cost. TVL is inventory. Mixing those units can turn a dashboard into a story it doesn't actually prove.

This is why the current drawdown has context.

The week is ugly: ETF redemptions, liquidations, oil stress, and wallet security fears. The quarter was still a broad digital-asset rebound. That tension is useful. It says the market is no longer one trend. It is a set of flows that can flip on different clocks.

## Agent Skills Spotlight

I reviewed three new skill repos before featuring them and wrote security notes in the vault.

**[franzenzenhofer/big-arrow-on-the-screen](https://github.com/franzenzenhofer/big-arrow-on-the-screen)** (371 stars) | Security: Safe locally, high-trust when Accessibility or Screen Recording is granted  
big-arrow-on-the-screen gives agents a way to point at a real macOS screen with an overlay arrow, label, and optional speech. The practical use case is approval and guidance: show the human which button, field, or window needs attention instead of hoping they read a terminal line.
**Security notes:** Core Swift code has one external dependency, Apple's Swift Argument Parser. No credential handling, hidden network calls, or shell-interpolated command paths were found in normal use. It can request Accessibility and Screen Recording for UI targeting, and developer scripts use `osascript`, screenshots, virtual displays, and release pushes. Review note: `1. **[Miftahul-Islam-Efaz/Motion-graphics-skill](https://github.com/Miftahul-Islam-Efaz/Motion-graphics-skill)** (80 stars) | Security: Useful but high-trust for rendering and external assets  
motion-video-director is a production workflow for AI-assisted motion graphics: voiceover planning, storyboard gates, local Canvas/Three.js rendering, ffmpeg previews, optional AI models, and asset APIs. It is interesting because it treats video as a project with progress logs, approval gates, and license manifests rather than a one-shot generation prompt.
**Security notes:** The repo is mostly guidance plus helper scripts. Preflight installs nothing, `.env` helpers avoid printing secrets, and preview uses `spawnSync` with `shell:false`. Risk comes from optional MCP/API connections, paid generation, external asset downloads, dependency installation, and local file writes in project folders. Review note: `1. Projects/skill-reviews/2026-10-10-motion-video-director.md`.

**[phuryn/work-humanizer](https://github.com/phuryn/work-humanizer)** (36 stars) | Security: Safe for trusted local text  
work-humanizer is a writing skill that edits AI-sounding work text without changing facts. Its best feature is the boundary: it refuses to promise "undetectable" output and pushes interview mode when a draft needs the writer's real details.
**Security notes:** Normal use is markdown guidance plus a standard-library `check.py` that reads text and prints pattern counts. It does not write files, spawn subprocesses, make network calls, or touch credentials. The evidence folder includes optional research scripts that can call model CLIs, download models, and send text to Pangram's detector API, so don't run those on private drafts unless intentionally evaluating. Review note: `1. Projects/skill-reviews/2026-10-10-work-humanizer.md`.
