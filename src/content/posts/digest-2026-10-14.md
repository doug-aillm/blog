## 1. Federated Hermes Put Tokenized Fund Shares On The 2027 Calendar

[CoinDesk reported](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/13/federated-hermes-joins-wall-street-s-tokenization-push-with-plans-for-first-onchain-fund-shares) that Federated Hermes, which oversees $911 billion, plans to issue its first tokenized fund share classes in early 2027.

The important detail is control. Federated has worked with third parties that tokenized existing products, but now it wants to issue tokenized fund shares itself. Prometheum would provide regulated brokerage, custody, clearing, and settlement.

This moves tokenization closer to the issuer's operating model.

Tokenized stocks were yesterday's visible story. Tokenized fund shares are duller and probably more practical. Money-market and liquidity products already behave like cash management tools. Put them onchain and the question becomes distribution: can advisers and brokerage accounts use the product without rebuilding their whole stack?

## 2. South Korea Blocked 27 Unregistered Exchange Websites

[CoinDesk reported](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/10/13/south-korea-blocks-27-crypto-exchanges-including-bitmart-and-kucoin) that South Korea is blocking web access to 27 unregistered exchanges, including KuCoin, BitMart, and MEXC.

The Financial Intelligence Unit had already restricted mobile-app access last year. This round extends the restriction to websites, and regulators warned that users on unauthorized platforms may struggle to recover funds.

This is Korea tightening the exits and entrances at the same time.

The country still has one of the biggest crypto user bases in Asia, but the regulator is making foreign venue access less casual. If users want offshore liquidity, they now have to weigh price, access, legal status, and withdrawal risk together.

## 3. Sberbank Got Approval To Trade Crypto

[CoinDesk reported](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/13/russia-s-largest-bank-sberbank-secures-approval-to-trade-crypto) that Russia's largest bank, Sberbank, joined the Bank of Russia's crypto exchange register.

Sberbank plans to open crypto trading to all clients on December 1 and is already testing BTC, ETH, and USDT trades in its app. The same system keeps domestic crypto payments banned, and from July 2027 transactions must run through registered intermediaries.

This is regulated adoption with a hard perimeter.

Russia isn't opening a free crypto market. It's moving trading and custody into banks, brokers, exchanges, and digital depositories. That gives users access while giving the state a much cleaner chokepoint.

## 4. Binance Is Shutting Down Mining Pool Services

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-10-13-binance-to-shut-down-mining-pool-cloud-mining-and-related-services-418410) that Binance will discontinue Binance Pool, Pool Savings, Cloud Mining, Transaction Accelerator, hashrate alerts, APIs, and related endpoints on November 10 at 07:00 UTC.

Users need to redirect miners and redeem active Pool Savings positions. Historical pages stay accessible for 14 days after the cutoff, then disappear.

This is a product-pruning signal.

Binance is putting more energy into AI trading interfaces, stock-linked derivatives, and traditional-finance products. Mining pools are closer to old crypto infrastructure. Closing them says the exchange sees more growth in distribution, data, and execution than in running hashrate services.

## 5. Ripple And Privy Brought Embedded Wallets To XRPL

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-10-13-stripe-privy-ripple-xrp-ledger-wallets-418463) that Ripple and Stripe-owned Privy partnered to add XRP Ledger support to Privy's wallet infrastructure.

Developers can create XRPL wallets, sign transactions, and support XRP, RLUSD, and other XRPL assets. Privy also plans policy controls and gas sponsorship over time.

This is a distribution story hiding inside a wallet story.

Embedded wallets make the chain disappear from the user's first interaction. That is useful for payments, stablecoins, and agent commerce, but it also shifts trust toward the app developer and wallet infrastructure provider. Private-key export helps. Policy controls matter even more.

## 6. Interactive Brokers Added ETH Staking Through Zerohash

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2026-10-13-interactive-brokers-ethereum-staking-zerohash-418457) that Interactive Brokers now lets eligible clients stake ETH through its Client Portal, with Zerohash providing the service.

Rewards are credited as additional staked ETH and can compound while the position remains staked. The launch follows the broker's earlier crypto trading, transfer, and 24/7 USDC funding moves.

This is staking moving into the same mental bucket as securities lending.

Retail and institutional brokerage clients don't need validator keys, withdrawal credentials, or staking-provider dashboards. They need a yield line item inside the account they already use. The risk doesn't vanish. It gets wrapped in brokerage UX.

## 7. DRW And Marex Traded A Tokenized Credit-Index Swap

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-10-13-drw-marex-first-otc-swap-tokenized-sp-iboxx-index-canton-network-418440) that DRW and Marex completed an OTC total return swap referencing a tokenized S&P iBoxx USD Liquid Investment Grade Index.

The trade ran on Derivnex's Canton-based platform, with Kaiko tokenizing the benchmark and providing official index values for valuation. Canton has also processed more than $350 billion in daily U.S. Treasury repo volume.

This is the boring part of tokenization that matters.

The tokenized object isn't a meme asset or a wrapper for a stock. It's a benchmark and a derivatives workflow. If benchmark data, licensing, valuation, and trade lifecycle can share one rail, tokenization starts to look like back-office compression rather than front-end speculation.

## 8. Ledger's Reseller Problem Reached European Retail

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-10-13-ledger-confirms-hardware-implant-cryptobilis-reseller-units-mediamarkt-suspends-sales-europe-418442) that Ledger confirmed unauthorized LTE implants in devices sold by CryptoBilis and said MediaMarkt Saturn suspended European Ledger sales after a German customer reported a tampered unit.

Ledger said the implant reads the 24-word recovery phrase during setup and transmits it over LTE. It also said its Secure Element, OS, transactions, signatures, infrastructure, systems, and services weren't compromised.

This is a supply-chain failure, not a normal wallet exploit.

Users often treat "buy hardware wallet" as the end of the security story. This incident says the purchase channel is part of the threat model. Firmware integrity matters. So does the box, the reseller, the device screen, and the moment a user copies seed words.

## 9. Strive Bought 3,311 Bitcoin

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-10-13-strive-buys-3311-bitcoin-280-million-largest-acquisition-since-january-418430) that Strive added 3,311 BTC between October 5 and October 9 for about $280 million.

That lifted holdings to 32,773 BTC. Strive paid about $84,500 per coin including fees and expenses, and said its cash balance also rose to $310.3 million.

This is corporate Bitcoin buying with a financing machine attached.

Strategy's weekly purchase was small. Strive's was large enough to matter for the public-holder leaderboard. The real question is less "who bought?" and more "what capital stack funded it?" Preferred shares, warrants, cash, and Bitcoin reserves are now one instrument family.

## 10. STX Rallied As Bitcoin Yield Got Attention

[CoinDesk's Daybook](https://www.coindesk.com/daybook-us/2026/10/13/stacks-stx-a-bitcoin-layer-2-token-has-surged-by-50-in-four-weeks) said STX, the native token of Bitcoin layer-2 project Stacks, rose nearly 10% in 24 hours and more than 50% in four weeks.

The cited driver is interest in Stacks' non-custodial Bitcoin staking architecture. The next bonding period in November requires participants to lock STX alongside BTC to secure capacity.

This is a better altcoin story than another generic rotation.

Bitcoin yield is usually wrapped, bridged, borrowed against, or centralized. Stacks is getting attention because the yield story stays closer to native BTC custody. That doesn't make it risk-free. It does explain why liquidity can rotate into a Bitcoin-adjacent token even while majors chop sideways.
