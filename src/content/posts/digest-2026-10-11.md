## 1. Ether ETF Redemptions Became A Cross-Asset Fund Signal

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-10-10-ether-etfs-extend-outflow-streak-to-nine-days-as-solana-funds-snap-record-14-week-inflow-run-418215) that U.S. spot ether ETFs lost $542.1 million last week, their worst week since January, while the products extended their outflow streak to nine trading days.

The same data showed Solana funds losing $24.8 million and snapping a 14-week inflow streak. Spot bitcoin ETFs also shed $681.1 million, ending three straight weeks of inflows. Across bitcoin, ether, and Solana, the weekly outflow total was about $1.25 billion.

This is more useful than another spot-price paragraph.

The October 10 evening update covered October ETF withdrawals as a BTC and ETH story. The new piece is breadth. Solana had been the relative-strength fund flow. When that streak breaks too, the read shifts from one asset losing sponsorship to crypto beta losing fund-wrapper support.

## 2. Kalshi Investigated Pre-Announcement Political Bets

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-10-10-kalshi-is-investigating-bets-on-trumps-press-secretary-pick-placed-before-announcement-wsj-418222) that Kalshi is investigating trades that correctly predicted President Donald Trump would name Katie Zacharia as White House press secretary before news outlets reported the pick.

The bets were small: about $173 across three wagers. The expected payout is not small relative to stake, at roughly $9,600 combined, because Zacharia had traded around 1% odds before the reports.

This is prediction markets hitting the information-quality wall.

The category wants to be treated as price discovery. That gets harder when thin political markets can be moved by people close to nonpublic personnel decisions. It does not mean the product is broken. It does mean surveillance, identity records, market sizing, and event design are no longer back-office details.

## 3. ESMA Asked Whether Tokenized Collateral Can Become Cash In A Crisis

[Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/esma-tokenized-collateral-eu-clearinghouses) that ESMA published a call for evidence on tokenized collateral for EU clearinghouses.

The question is blunt: can a clearinghouse access tokenized collateral and convert it into cash under stress? ESMA is seeking feedback on legal, liquidity, and operational risks before deciding whether more EU rules are needed.

This is tokenization meeting market plumbing.

The easy tokenization pitch is faster settlement and cleaner records. Clearing asks the scarier question: what happens when a member fails, markets are gapping, and the collateral has to be liquidated right now? Tokenized assets do not become high-quality collateral just because the record is programmable.

## 4. France Moved Stablecoin Swaps Toward Taxable Events

[Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/france-crypto-stablecoin-swap-tax-2027-budget) that the French National Assembly Finance Committee backed proposals to tax conversions into fiat-pegged stablecoins and extend exit-tax rules to crypto investors.

One adopted amendment would make crypto-to-stablecoin conversions taxable from January 1, 2027. Another would cover unrealized gains when households with more than 800,000 euros of crypto move their tax residence abroad. A separate provision would let investors carry realized crypto losses forward for 10 years.

This is a tax read on stablecoins as cash equivalents.

If a USDC or EURC conversion crystallizes a gain, stablecoin routing stops being a neutral parking step. Portfolio tools, wallets, exchanges, and tax software will need to model tax timing before users move between volatile tokens and fiat-pegged balances.

## 5. France Recorded 90 Crypto-Linked Physical Attacks In Seven Months

[Cointelegraph Magazine reported](https://cointelegraph.com/magazine/france-is-worlds-most-dangerous-place-for-bitcoiners-90-attacks-in-7-months) that French authorities recorded 90 crypto-related cases involving kidnapping, abduction, extortion, threats, and violent theft from January 1 to mid-August 2026.

The Interior Ministry data also included 223 arrests and 126 imprisonments from January to July. The report said the case count works out to a new incident about every two and a half days, though France does not have a year-on-year comparison because it began recording these events in 2026.

This is the custody story outside the wallet.

Multisigs, hardware devices, and policy engines do not solve the problem if wealth becomes publicly attributable to a person and location. Privacy, operational security, travel routines, social posting, and inheritance design are now part of crypto risk management.

## 6. Tron Said Post-Quantum Cryptography Is Live On Testnet

[Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/justin-sun-says-trons-post-quantum-cryptography-has-gone-live-on-testnet) that Justin Sun said post-quantum cryptography is live on the Tron testnet.

Sun said the network is tracking quantum computing and AI progress and is ready to bring quantum resistance to mainnet when needed. The announcement follows a week where Zcash developers also set a January target for quantum-resistant payment support.

This is no longer a one-chain worry.

The practical question is not whether Q-Day is tomorrow. It is whether chains can rehearse migration paths before fear forces bad operational choices. Testnets are where those trade-offs should be exposed.

## 7. Crypto Lending Rebounded, But The Risk Stack Got Stranger

[Cointelegraph Magazine reported](https://cointelegraph.com/magazine/crypto-lending-rises-again-but-have-they-solved-the-risks) that crypto lending total value locked has risen more than 55% since the start of July to about $56 billion.

The rebound follows a rough second quarter, when Galaxy figures cited by Cointelegraph showed $11.33 billion leaving the sector. The report framed the new risk mix around AI-assisted hacks and cascading failures across linked protocols.

This is the credit cycle trying again with more automation around it.

DeFi lending has always had oracle, liquidation, collateral, and governance risk. The newer problem is composability plus speed. If attack discovery, exploit routing, and leveraged exit behavior become more automated, risk controls have less time to be human.

## 8. Coinbase Opened A Regulated Deribit Route For U.S. Institutions

[Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/coinbase-deribit-us-crypto-options-perpetual-futures) that Coinbase completed its Deribit integration, giving eligible U.S. customers access to global crypto derivatives liquidity through Coinbase Financial Markets.

The derivatives unit is regulated by the CFTC. The initial focus is U.S. institutions, with U.S. retail options expected later this year.

This is offshore derivatives liquidity moving into a U.S. regulatory wrapper.

Deribit built the crypto options venue that institutions already watch. Coinbase is trying to make that liquidity usable without making U.S. customers route around the perimeter. The winner here may not be the venue with the most exotic product. It may be the venue that can normalize access.

## 9. Circle Pushed USDC And EURC Into SAP-Linked Enterprise Payments

[Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/circle-usdc-eurc-payments-sap-enterprise-ecosystem) that Circle and SAP-backed Tereina will integrate USDC and EURC into enterprise payment workflows, starting with SAP Cloud ERP.

The integration will be available through SAP Pay for eligible customers. USDC is the preferred stablecoin for dollar-denominated workflows, while EURC is available for euro-denominated payments.

This is the enterprise stablecoin path that matters.

Treasury teams do not want a separate crypto tool if the payment can sit inside software they already use. The adoption question moves from whether companies will touch stablecoins to whether stablecoin rails disappear into the accounts-payable stack.

## 10. Tether And Kazakhstan Studied A Tenge Stablecoin Pilot

[Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/tether-kazakhstan-central-bank-to-explore-tenge-stablecoin-and-asset-tokenization) that Tether signed an agreement with the National Bank of Kazakhstan and the Alatau City Authority to explore a tenge-pegged stablecoin and tokenized real-world assets.

The memorandum covers international issuance models, possible use cases, a tenge-backed stablecoin pilot, and an asset-tokenization framework that could use the Tether Hadron platform.

This is the non-dollar stablecoin story from another angle.

Dollar stablecoins dominate liquidity. Local-currency pilots test a different promise: settlement, treasury, and asset records that match domestic money and domestic policy goals. The hard part is whether users care enough when dollar liquidity is already everywhere.
