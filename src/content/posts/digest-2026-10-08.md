## 1. The CFTC Started Building A Crypto Rulebook Without CLARITY

[Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/cftc-proposed-crypto-framework-failed-clarity-vote) that CFTC Chair Michael Selig announced a crypto framework after the CLARITY Act failed to advance.

The agency is seeking comment on rules for companies offering retail customers margined, leveraged, or financed crypto transactions. Selig called the route `CTX`. The plan also considers a new `crypto asset market` category under the CFTC's designated contract market regime, giving some exchanges a national registration path.

This is the post-CLARITY phase getting real.

The industry wanted a statute that could survive elections. It may get agency rules first. That's faster, and it's more fragile. A CFTC pathway can reduce state-by-state friction for some venues. It still leaves spot exchanges, securities questions, custody, and DeFi interfaces in a messier zone.

## 2. Better Markets Said The CFTC Is The Wrong Retail Crypto Regulator

[Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/better-markets-criticize-cftc-regulate-retail-crypto) that Better Markets criticized the CFTC's proposed framework for retail crypto transactions.

The group's securities-policy director argued that the CFTC lacks the investor-protection mandate that defines the SEC. He also said the cited statutory authority was built around leveraged precious-metals fraud, not a broad retail crypto market. Better Markets warned that the framework could allow affiliations between market participants that echo some FTX-era conflict risks.

This is more than a turf fight.

The policy split decides what kind of user the law sees. If crypto is mostly a commodity market, the CFTC's exchange and derivatives model fits better. If retail users need securities-style disclosures and conflict controls, the SEC has the stronger claim. Apps building leverage, tokenized stocks, perps, and wallet-native execution need to know which assumption wins.

## 3. Rain Joined The National Trust Bank Queue

[Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/rain-seeks-us-trust-bank-charter-days-after-occ-sued-over-crypto-charters) that stablecoin payments infrastructure provider Rain applied to form Rain National Trust Bank in New York.

If approved, the bank could provide fiduciary custody for digital assets and dollars, manage reserves for permitted stablecoin issuers, and issue or redeem dollar-backed stablecoins under the GENIUS Act. The filing arrived days after the Independent Community Bankers of America sued the OCC over trust-bank rules for crypto firms.

This is stablecoin infrastructure trying to become boring finance.

The charter route gives customers a federal fiduciary to point at. Community banks see a different story: crypto firms getting bank-like branding without the same obligations as deposit-taking banks. That fight matters because "national trust bank" may become the wrapper that lets stablecoin programs sell compliance as part of the product.

## 4. Modern Treasury Asked For Its Own Trust Bank

[Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/modern-treasury-seeks-us-trust-bank-charter-for-stablecoin-custody) that Modern Treasury applied to create Modern Treasury National Trust Bank.

The proposed limited-purpose bank would custody stablecoins and fiat for customers, but it wouldn't issue stablecoins or make loans. Modern Treasury said its existing payments platform has facilitated more than $600 billion across hundreds of organizations. The application puts it beside Circle, BitGo, Payward, Zerohash, Block, Ripple, Bastion, and Rain in the national-charter race.

This is the payments stack moving closer to regulated custody.

Modern Treasury is pitching operating infrastructure for companies that already move money. That makes the trust-bank application important. Stablecoins become easier to use when custody, fiat movement, ledgering, and compliance sit inside one service surface.

## 5. Robinhood Put $25 Million Of Bitcoin On Its Own Balance Sheet

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-10-07-robinhood-adds-25-million-bitcoin-balance-sheet-417890) that Robinhood added $25 million worth of Bitcoin to its corporate balance sheet.

Johann Kerbrat, Robinhood's crypto and international head, said the purchase was meant to signal commitment to Bitcoin and the wider crypto sector. The dollar amount is tiny beside Robinhood's public-market value. Still, it's the first time the trading app has made Bitcoin a corporate treasury asset.

This is a brand signal more than a treasury strategy.

Strategy buys Bitcoin to define its capital structure. Robinhood bought Bitcoin to align the company with the asset it sells to users. That distinction matters. If brokerages, wallets, and exchanges start holding small corporate positions, the treasury trade becomes less about leverage and more about credibility with the user base.

## 6. Jito's JTX Is Moving Toward Mobile Trading And Perps

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-10-07-jitos-jtx-plans-mobile-app-this-fall-eyes-perps-integration-later-this-winter-417905) that Jito's JTX plans a native mobile app later this fall and may integrate perpetual futures later in the winter.

Jito Foundation President Brian Smith said JTX is focused on spot features first. The Solana-based self-custodial trading app launched in July with spot trading for cbBTC, SOL, HYPE, memecoins, tokenized equities, and ETFs. It also plans an equities feature within two weeks and an onchain data-discovery feature.

This is Solana trading infrastructure moving toward the phone.

The key shift is self-custodial execution trying to meet retail users where they already trade. If JTX can make spot, tokenized equities, data discovery, and later perps feel like one mobile product, Solana gets an app-layer answer to centralized brokerage UX.

## 7. Hyperliquid's Policy Center Pushed The Public-Chain Exchange Thesis

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-10-07-hyperliquid-policy-center-jake-chervinsky-interview-417896) that Hyperliquid Policy Center CEO Jake Chervinsky argued every major exchange may need public blockchain infrastructure within a decade.

He framed Hyperliquid as infrastructure that crypto-native and traditional exchanges could use, rather than a venue meant to replace Coinbase, Kraken, CME, ICE, or Kalshi. He also said onchain markets need a path into the U.S. regulatory perimeter.

This is the exchange debate moving down the stack.

The harder claim is that public-chain settlement, transparent execution, and always-on collateral become table stakes for any exchange that wants to compete. If that thesis is right, regulation has to separate protocol infrastructure from the apps and firms using it.

## 8. Moody's Rated Sky Protocol B3

[The Block reported](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-10-07-moodys-sky-protocol-b3-rating-first-stablecoin-protocol-417931) that Moody's assigned Sky Protocol a B3 issuer rating with a stable outlook.

Sky said the rating makes it the only stablecoin protocol formally rated by both Moody's and S&P. Galaxy recently added $100 million of sUSDS to its corporate treasury and approved sUSDS as collateral across its institutional trading business. Standard Chartered has also deepened ties with the protocol.

This is DeFi credit getting translated for institutions.

USDS may live onchain, but rating agencies speak the language of risk committees. A B3 rating isn't a victory lap. It's a bridge. Reserves, capital position, collateral use, treasury allocation, and protocol governance become legible enough for institutions to discuss without pretending DeFi risk is the same as bank risk.

## 9. The ECB Warned Tokenization Could Fragment Without A Digital Euro

[Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/european-central-bank-fragmentation-digital-euro) that ECB Executive Board member Piero Cipollone warned tokenization platforms could fragment Europe's payment system without a pan-European digital payment solution.

Cipollone said a digital euro would not take over banks' role, but would give banks infrastructure to compete in the digital age. The ECB has not decided whether to issue one, but it wants the legislative process finished by the end of 2026. A 12-month pilot could start in the second half of 2027, with possible issuance in 2029.

This is Europe's answer to stablecoin gravity.

The U.S. path is letting dollar stablecoins and trust-chartered firms move fast. Europe's path is trying to keep public money and banks at the center of tokenized payments. The tension is obvious: too much central control slows adoption, but too little shared infrastructure lets private platforms define the rails.

## 10. Crypto PACs Are Repricing The Midterm Map

[AP reported](https://apnews.com/article/f8d9d14e21b0bf95c46b516f8ac5f14b) that a major cryptocurrency campaign organization plans to support a bipartisan slate of House candidates after market-structure legislation stalled.

The broader signal is that crypto political spending is moving from single-bill pressure to election-cycle positioning. The industry has already spent years pushing for market-structure law, stablecoin rules, and clearer agency boundaries. With CLARITY stalled, campaign support becomes a way to shape the next Congress before the next serious legislative window.

This is regulation by calendar.

Crypto firms can lobby agencies this quarter, but durable statutes need votes after the midterms. That gives PACs a simple target: protect members who backed market-structure work and pressure the ones who blocked it. Policy may feel procedural, but it's becoming a direct input into token listings, exchange models, custody products, and stablecoin distribution.
